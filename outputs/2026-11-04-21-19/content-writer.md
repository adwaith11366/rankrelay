# How much does budget travel cost per day?

Budget travel has no single daily price. Your cost per day is the sum of four lines: a bed, food, local transport and activities. Add current prices for your destination, then add a buffer. This guide shows how to do that in about ten minutes.

We don't quote a dollar figure here. Costs change by country, city and season, and we haven't sourced a number we could stand behind. The method works with whatever prices you find today.

## Line one: where you sleep

Your bed is usually the largest single line, so fix it first. Look at dorm beds, private rooms in guesthouses and cheap hotel rooms for the city you'll be in. Write down the lowest price you'd honestly accept, not the lowest price on the page.

If you're travelling alone, our [hostel vs guesthouse comparison](https://adwaith11366.github.io/rankrelay/hostel-vs-guesthouse.html) covers when a private room is the better deal.

## Line two: food

Decide how many meals you'll buy and how many you'll make or grab cheaply. A traveller who cooks breakfast in a hostel kitchen and eats street food at lunch has a very different line from one who sits down three times a day.

Check a few menus or market prices for your destination. Then multiply by your meal count. Our page on [eating on a budget](https://adwaith11366.github.io/rankrelay/eat-on-a-budget.html) has more on keeping this line low.

## Line three: getting around locally

This covers buses, metros, shared taxis and the occasional ride you can't avoid. Look up a single fare and a day pass, if one exists. Then estimate how many trips you'll make in a normal day. Walking costs nothing, so a compact city gives you a smaller line than a sprawling one.

## Line four: things to do

List the two or three sights you'd regret missing and look up their entry prices. Spread them across your days. Many places have free days, free walking tours or free viewpoints, and those bring the average down.

## Add them up, then add a buffer

Add the four lines to get a daily base. Then add a cushion for the things you can't plan: a missed bus, a lost charger, a rainy day that pushes you indoors. We'd rather you over-budget a little than cut the trip short, but the right size for the cushion depends on you. If you've travelled the region before, it can be small. If it's your first trip, make it bigger.

## What stays outside the daily number

Keep flights, visas, insurance and gear out of the daily figure. They're one-off costs, and mixing them in makes your daily number look bigger or smaller than it really is. For flights, see [when to book](https://adwaith11366.github.io/rankrelay/cheap-flights-timing.html).

Multiply the daily number by the length of your trip, then add the one-off costs. That total is your trip budget.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I work out a daily budget for a trip?

Add four lines for your destination: accommodation, food, local transport and activities. Use current prices for each, add a buffer for surprises, and you have a daily budget.

### Should I include flights in my daily budget?

No. Flights are a one-off cost. Keep them separate, work out your daily number on its own, and add the flight cost to the total trip budget.

### How big should my buffer be?

It depends on your experience. If you know the region and your costs are mostly fixed, a small buffer is enough. On a first trip, or somewhere you can't predict prices, leave a bigger one.
