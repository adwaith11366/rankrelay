# Is travel insurance worth it on a budget trip?

Yes, if losing the trip or paying a foreign hospital bill would hurt you. No, if you have booked refundable everything, you are travelling close to home, and you could absorb an emergency from savings. Most budget travellers land on a cheap, narrow policy: medical cover first, cancellation cover second, and almost nothing else.

## The short test

Ask two questions.

First: what do I lose if I can't go? Add up everything you have paid that you can't get back: flights, tours, prepaid stays. If that total would sting, cancellation cover earns its fee.

Second: what happens if I get hurt abroad? If you have no way to pay for treatment or a flight home, you need medical cover. This is the part of travel insurance that budget trips most often skip, and it is the part that can cost the most.

If both answers are "not much," you can reasonably go without.

## What a policy usually covers

Policies differ, so read the one you are offered. Most include some mix of these:

- Medical treatment abroad, and sometimes transport home
- Trip cancellation or interruption for listed reasons
- Delayed or lost baggage
- Theft of belongings, usually with limits on single items

Cancellation cover pays only for reasons the policy names, such as illness. Changing your mind is rarely one of them. Read the exclusions before you pay.

## Check your credit card first

Some credit cards include travel cover when you pay for the trip with them. Others include none. The terms vary by card, country and account level, so look up your card's benefits guide and read it, or call the issuer.

Look for four things: whether it covers medical costs or only trip delays, whether you must book the whole trip on the card, what the limits are, and whether it is the main cover or pays only after another policy.

If the card covers medical costs and your trip is cheap, you may need nothing more. If it covers only delays and baggage, you still have a gap.

## When to skip it

Skip insurance when you are travelling by car or train within your own country, when every booking is refundable, or when your health system covers you where you are going and you have confirmed that in writing.

## When to buy it

Buy it when you have prepaid flights or tours you can't change, when you are going somewhere with expensive or limited healthcare, when you will do anything physical such as hiking or scooter riding (check that the policy covers it), or when you have a health condition that could flare up.

Buy early. Many policies cover cancellation only if you buy soon after you make your first payment, so waiting until the week before can remove that benefit. Ask the insurer about its deadline.

## How to buy cheaply

Get quotes for the same dates and the same coverage from two providers. Set a low limit on belongings if you carry little of value. Pick a higher excess (the amount you pay before the policy does) only if you could afford it on the day. Then save the policy number and the emergency phone line offline.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is travel insurance worth it for a cheap trip?

It depends on what you can't afford to lose. A cheap trip can still carry a large medical risk, so medical cover is worth considering even when the flights were low-cost. Cancellation cover matters more as your nonrefundable bookings grow.

### Does my credit card cover travel insurance?

Some cards do and some don't. Coverage depends on the card, the issuer and how you paid for the trip. Read your card's benefits guide or ask the issuer whether it covers medical costs, cancellation, or only delays and baggage.

### When should I buy travel insurance?

Soon after you make your first nonrefundable payment. Some cancellation benefits depend on buying within a set period, so check your insurer's deadline before you wait.
