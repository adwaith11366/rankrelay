# Do you need travel insurance on a budget trip?

Yes, in most cases. If a single medical bill, a lost bag or a cancelled flight would wreck your budget, you need cover. If you could absorb the loss without borrowing money, you can make a choice. That is the whole test.

Budget travellers tend to skip insurance because it feels like money spent on nothing. The logic runs backwards. A traveller with a thin budget has the least room to absorb a surprise, so a surprise hurts more.

## What travel insurance covers

Policies differ, so read the one you are offered. Most travel policies fall into a few areas:

- Medical treatment abroad, and sometimes getting you home if you need it.
- Trip cancellation or interruption, such as an illness before you leave.
- Lost, stolen or damaged luggage and belongings.
- Delays and missed connections.

Each area has limits and exclusions. Pre-existing conditions, risky activities and travel against government advice are common sticking points. Check the policy wording for each before you pay.

## The one part you shouldn't skimp on

Medical cover is the part that matters most. Treatment abroad can be expensive, and a bad injury or illness may need a transfer to another hospital or a flight home. A stolen phone is annoying; a hospital bill you can't pay is a different problem.

If you're cutting cost, cut the extras first: gadget cover, high luggage limits, add-ons for sports you won't play. Keep the medical section.

## When you may not need to buy it

Look at what you already have before you buy a separate policy. Three places to check:

- Your credit or debit card. Some cards include travel cover if you pay for the trip with them. Conditions vary by card and country, so read the terms.
- Your health insurance or national health scheme. Some give limited cover abroad, and some give none.
- Your employer or a bank account package.

Ask each provider what is covered, for how long, and what you must do to activate it. Existing cover often has gaps, such as a cap on trip length or no cover for cancellation. If a gap is one you can't afford, buy a policy that fills it.

## When insurance is required

A few countries and visa types ask for proof of cover at the border or during the application. Check the official entry requirements for every country on your route. See our guide to visa basics for where to start.

## How to buy cover without overpaying

Get quotes from more than one provider and compare the same things: medical limit, cancellation limit, excess and exclusions. A cheaper policy with a low medical limit isn't cheaper in any way that counts.

Buy early. Cancellation cover usually applies only to events after you buy, so a policy bought at the airport can't cover a problem that started last week. If you're booking a flight, read our guide to when to book cheap flights, then buy insurance soon after you pay.

Keep the policy number and the provider's emergency phone number on your phone and on paper.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is travel insurance worth it for a short, cheap trip?

It depends on what you would lose. A short trip carries the same medical risk as a long one, and a cheap flight can still be non-refundable. If you couldn't pay for an emergency yourself, it's worth having.

### Does my credit card already cover me?

Some cards do, but terms vary. Check whether you must pay for the trip with the card, how long the cover lasts, and whether it includes medical costs. Ask your card provider for the policy document.

### What's the one thing I shouldn't skip?

Medical cover. Drop the optional extras if you need to save money, but keep enough medical cover for the countries you're visiting.
