# Is travel insurance worth it on a budget trip?

Yes, for most trips. A budget trip leaves you with little spare cash, and one hospital bill or one missed flight can cost more than the rest of the trip put together. Insurance is the one expense where spending a little protects everything else you've saved.

You can reasonably skip it on a short, cheap, refundable trip close to home, where you're covered by other insurance you've already checked. For anything else, buy it.

## What basic cover pays for

Most policies bundle four things:

- Emergency medical care and, in some policies, getting you home if you're too ill to fly.
- Trip cancellation or interruption, if you have to cancel for a reason the policy names.
- Delayed or lost baggage.
- Theft of gear, up to a limit per item.

Medical cover is the part to care about most. Everything else costs you money if it goes wrong; a medical emergency abroad can cost you far more than money.

## When you can skip it

Skip it if all of these are true: the trip is short, every booking is refundable, you're in a country where your home health cover works, and you carry nothing you couldn't afford to lose. That's a narrow list, and a backpacking trip rarely fits it.

Check before you rely on a bank card. Some cards include travel cover, but the terms vary by card and country. Read the actual document, and look for the medical limit, the list of exclusions and whether you had to pay for the trip with that card.

## What to check before you buy

Cheap policies are cheap for a reason. Read these parts before you pay:

- The medical limit. A low limit can run out in a single hospital stay.
- Activities. Many policies exclude things budget travellers do, such as motorbike riding, trekking at altitude or diving. If you plan any of them, check the policy names them.
- Pre-existing conditions. These are often excluded unless you declare them.
- The excess. This is the amount you pay first on every claim.
- Cancellation reasons. Changing your mind is almost never covered.
- Claim rules. Most insurers want a police report for theft and receipts for everything.

Compare two or three policies on those six points, not on the headline price. We don't quote prices here because they depend on your age, destination and trip length.

## When to buy

Buy when you book your flights, not the night before you leave. Cancellation cover only helps for events that happen after you've bought the policy. If you've just booked a fare using our guide to booking flights at the right time, this is the next step.

## Questions travellers ask

### Is travel insurance worth it if I'm only going for a week?

Yes, if you have non-refundable bookings or aren't covered for medical care in that country. A short trip is as exposed to an accident or a cancelled flight as a long one.

### Does travel insurance cover a cancelled flight?

Sometimes. Policies cover cancellation only for reasons they list, and the airline may owe you a refund or rebooking first. Read the cancellation section and keep every message from the airline.

### Can I buy travel insurance after I've left home?

Some insurers sell to travellers already abroad, but the terms are often narrower, and you usually can't claim for anything that started before you bought. Buy before you go.
