## How this list was built

I read lead-researcher.md and content-writer.md first. The article to promote is the unpublished draft "Do you need travel insurance on a budget trip?", planned for /travel-insurance-basics.html (currently a 404 on the site).

WebSearch worked. I ran four searches (budget travel resource pages, budget travel blog roundups, travel insurance for budget travelers, solo travel community wikis). Every URL below was returned by one of those searches. I did not open the pages themselves, so "why it fits" comes from the search result titles and summaries. A person should open each page and confirm it is live, current and takes outside suggestions before anyone writes to it. Contact details, traffic and authority figures: n/a for every site, because I fetched none.

## Notes on the list

Five of the eight are resource or link pages, where a listing is a realistic ask. Two are roundups and are harder to get into. Two are insurance pages that could cite the guide. Several search results were also thin or off-topic (sales pages, mirrors of Reddit, stub pages), and I left them out. I also saw the r/solotravel wiki, which has a "Travel insurance 101" page, but only through unofficial mirror URLs, so I did not list it. Reddit communities usually restrict self-promotion, so that route needs a human to read the rules first.

All eight pitches wait until the article is published. Until then the email says it is coming soon.

## Outreach email: draft, not sent

Use for the resource and link pages (Desk to Dirtbag, TravelNotes.org, Transitions Abroad, Edinformatics). A person adds the recipient, the greeting name and the sender name before sending. No URL for the guide is included because it does not exist yet.

Subject: A budget travel insurance guide, coming soon from Shoestring Atlas

Hello,

I write for Shoestring Atlas, a budget travel blog. I found your budget travel resources page and wanted to introduce a guide we're finishing.

It's called "Do you need travel insurance on a budget trip?" and it will be published soon. It covers what travel insurance usually pays for (medical costs abroad, trip cancellation, lost or stolen baggage, delays) and what it usually leaves out, such as pre-existing conditions and risky activities you haven't added. It also walks through three places a traveler may already have cover: a credit or debit card, a home health plan, and an employer or household policy. It ends with how to compare policies on payout limits and excess instead of headline price, plus a short FAQ.

The guide doesn't name or rank any insurers.

If it suits your page, I'll send the link on the day it goes live so you can judge it first. If it doesn't fit, no reply is needed.

Thanks for reading,
The Shoestring Atlas team
